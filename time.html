Thesium Time

The bottleneck isn't building automations. It's noticing.

Most repeatable work is never recognized as repeatable. You do it, close the tab, and never think about it again. Thesium Time reads your own activity, finds the patterns, and hands each one back as an automation you can run.

In design · specification stage

Follow the work

The wedge

Discovery from your data, not a template gallery.

Workflow tools start at "pick a trigger." That only helps once you already know what to automate. Thesium Time starts from the other end: here is a thing you did eleven times last month, here are the events that prove it, here is what it cost you.

The signal that matters most is the prompt you keep re-pasting into an agent. Weekly roll-ups, PR description rewrites, triage summaries — work you have already automated by hand, every time, without ever packaging it.

Surfaces

Discovery, Builds, Watchtower.

Discovery

Patterns ranked by frequency, time cost, and how automatable they look — each one showing the observed events that justify it. You never have to trust an unexplained suggestion.

Builds

A candidate rendered into a runnable workflow that executes in the app, or into a Claude or Codex skill package you install in the runtime you already work in.

Watchtower

Patterns that emerge as your work shifts, visibility into what you have already automated, and recovery of your own past commentary before it decays out of reach.

Safety

A staircase, not autonomy by default.

An automation that runs against the wrong input damages real data in real systems. Execution is gated in four steps and nothing skips ahead.

Suggest

The candidate is described and simulated. Nothing executes.

Confirm per step

It runs, and every step that touches anything asks first.

Scheduled with approval

It runs on a schedule; effects still wait on your approval.

A fourth step — autonomous execution — is reachable only after a run history you can inspect.
