The method

Read, model, project.

The same pipeline runs in every product. Its stages are the reason the output can be checked rather than believed.

Read

Artifacts are ingested and normalized into a typed event and evidence store. Every item keeps the source it came from and the time it was published.

Model

Retrieval over a knowledge graph builds a model of the subject — a portfolio, a career, a working week. Small, focused queries do the work; no frontier model sits in the hot path, which is what keeps the cost per user predictable.

Project

A lens applies the model to one decision and returns the answer alongside the evidence that produced it. Results resting on stale or proxy data are labeled as such.

Underneath

Ownership is a model, not a promise.

"Your data stays yours" is easy to write on a marketing page and hard to hold up once an employer, a manager, or an agent enters the picture. Thesium rests on a published architecture — Ariadne — that answers it structurally.

People are tenants. Every record has exactly one owner tenant. Membership is a relation, never containment — so joining an organization writes one tuple and leaving deletes it, and no data moves in either direction. Offboarding is not a migration, because nothing was ever moved in.

Posture

What Thesium does not do.

These are properties of how the products are built, not settings you have to find and switch on.

  • No telemetry, analytics, or advertising identifiers.
  • No server-side copy of your holdings, documents, or activity.
  • No managed inference. Where a product uses a model, you supply it.
  • No data sold, shared, or used to train anything.
  • No unlabeled estimates — low-confidence output says so.
  • No autonomous action without an approval step you control.
